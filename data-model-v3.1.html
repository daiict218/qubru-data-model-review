Qubru PLM — Data Model

Domain-by-domain entity specification. A governed system of memory for the P&C policy lifecycle — 44 entities across 9 domains, traced end to end through one running example.

Running example

Pedro sells AXA insurance at a broker called AON. The AON relationship is managed by Martín, who works at AXA. Pedro sold a commercial/group policy to Transportes Andinos, insuring 14 tractor units. Each tractor carries four coverages: physical damage, third-party liability, theft, and personal injury. Juan, one of the drivers, had an accident and crashed his tractor, injuring María, a pedestrian.

This example runs through every domain in this document. Read the narrative walkthrough below first — it tells the whole story end to end and shows why each domain exists. Then the per-domain specifications give the precise attributes and relationships.

Why this model exists — read this first

For the technical co-founder. Before the entity tables, understand what PLM is doing and why each piece of the model earns its place.

The problem in one paragraph

In P&C insurance, underwriting, pricing, and claims have matured as separate disciplines with separate tools (Earnix for pricing, Guidewire for claims, UW workbenches). The cycle that should connect them is broken: the reasoning that ought to flow from claims outcomes back to pricing and underwriting is lost, because most of it lives as unstructured documents — memos, emails, FNOL narratives, peritajes — that no system has organized as queryable knowledge. PLM is the missing layer. It turns that unstructured lifecycle into governed institutional memory so the cycle can close. The category boundary is structural: PLM provides insight, the human decides, the core executes.

This data model is the schema of that memory. Every entity exists to answer a real question the carrier needs answered, or to make another answer auditable. Below, we follow one account — Transportes Andinos — from the moment Pedro pitches the policy to the moment the Superfinanciera asks about a claim a year later. Each step lights up a domain.

The story, domain by domain

Act 1 — The people and the relationship (Domain 1: Identity & Party)

Seven real-world actors enter the story: AXA (the carrier, the PLM tenant), AON (the broker), Transportes Andinos (the customer), and four people — Martín (AXA underwriter who manages the AON relationship), Pedro (AON salesperson), Juan (a driver), María (a pedestrian, not yet involved).

The first design decision is the one most data models get wrong: we separate who someone is from what role they play. AXA, AON, Transportes Andinos, Martín, Pedro, Juan, María each exist exactly once as a Party — pure identity, nothing else. Their roles are contextual and live elsewhere:

  • Transportes Andinos plays the Insured role and anchors an Account (the commercial relationship with AXA, placed through AON).
  • AON plays the Broker role.
  • Martín is an Actor — someone who acts inside PLM (binds policies, sets reserves). Crucially, AI agents are also Actors. This is what makes every machine-produced fact traceable to "who" produced it.
  • Pedro, Juan, and María are Parties with no permanent role — they appear later as a producer on a submission, a driver on a claim, an injured third party on a claim. We do not create Driver or Claimant subtypes; those are contextual involvements recorded where they happen.

Why this matters for the build: when Juan shows up in three different claims over two years, the question "how loss-making is this driver?" is a graph traversal from one Party node — not a reconciliation across three string fields named "driver_name". We deleted two entities from the old model here (Principal → a Party-to-Party relationship; Driver → a claim involvement) precisely because they were rigid roles masquerading as identities. ACORD's Party/Role separation validates this; the 2023 ACORD update even adds "virtual parties (AI systems)" — which is exactly our Actor.

Act 2 — The product the carrier sells (Domain 2: Product & Master Data)

Before Pedro can sell anything, AXA's product and actuarial teams have designed the auto product. This is Product (stable: "Seguro de Automóviles Colombia") and its ProductVersion (everything that changes over time). The version is the load-bearing idea: when AXA raises the theft factor 25% in March, that's ProductVersion 2024.2 with effective_from/effective_to. A policy bound in February is forever tied to 2024.1 — which is the only way to later answer "what were the rules when we bound this?"

Each version defines its Coverages (the four on the tractors), its RatingMatrix (the pricing structure — metadata about it, not the engine itself), its UWRuleset (appetite and authority rules), and its CommissionSchedule (AON earns 15% base + 2% if loss ratio stays under 65%). We added CommissionSchedule deliberately: without it you cannot compute the expense side of combined ratio, and combined ratio is the entire commercial argument of PLM.

Why this matters for the build: this domain is what makes profitability answerable over time. "Which products are profitable, how did that change, why, and which coverages drove it?" is a traversal from Product → ProductVersion → (Coverage + RatingMatrix + CommissionSchedule) → Policy → outcomes. The change_summary on each version tells you what changed; the claims data tells you whether it worked.

Act 3 — Where knowledge lives (Domain 3: Containers & Reference)

PLM's agents reason like a judge: they need evidence (what happened) and the law (how to evaluate it). Container separates these. The master container holds lifecycle evidence — Transportes Andinos' submission, its policy, its claims. The domain subjects (pricing, UW, claims) hold authored reference material — the rating matrix spreadsheet, the appetite-guide PDF, the reserve model. Each authored document is a ReferenceDocument with full upload provenance.

Why this matters for the build: these two kinds of content have opposite lifecycles (continuous arrival vs deliberate authoring) and different governance (retention by jurisdiction vs versioning by effective date). Conflating them forces the wrong discipline on both. The RatingMatrix in Domain 2 points to its ReferenceDocument here via source_reference_doc_id — structured metadata on one side, the actual file on the other.

Act 4 — The sale (Domain 4: Quote & Application)

Pedro submits the risk: 14 tractors, intercity freight, zone A, average age 8 years, 2 prior claims. That's a Submission. Martín prices it against RatingMatrix 2024.1 and issues a Quote at COP 80M. Pedro counter-proposes; Martín revises to COP 80M with a higher theft deductible; Pedro accepts.

The non-obvious decision: submissions that never become policies are kept. A declined submission ("Flota Caribe, 22 tractors, age 12 — outside appetite") is evidence of risk selection, and it's exactly what Query 2.2 (similar risks) needs. If submissions lived inside Policy, the declines would have nowhere to exist.

Why this matters for the build: the Submission carries the quote-time signals that Query 2.1 correlates with outcomes. The Quote records which RatingMatrix produced the premium — the first link in the bitemporal chain.

Act 5 — The contract (Domain 5: Policy Lifecycle)

Pedro accepts, Martín binds. Now the model fills in: the Policy (COP 80M, 14 units, bound under ProductVersion 2024.1, commission via the AON schedule, Martín's uw_rationale captured); 14 InsuredItems (each tractor with VIN, make, age, use class); 4 PolicyCoverages (the product coverages instantiated with real limits and deductibles, each carrying its slice of premium); Exposure records (vehicle-months and earned premium accruing monthly); and a PolicyTransaction for the bind. In June, Transportes Andinos adds 2 tractors — that's an Endorsement (premium 80M → 91.4M, two new InsuredItems, exposure starting in June).

Why this matters for the build: this is the spine. allocated_premium on PolicyCoverage lets you compute loss ratio per coverage. Time-bounded Exposure lets you compute earned premium for mid-term changes (the two June tractors don't get charged for January–May). The renewal chain (prior_policy_id) is a linked list that answers "show me five years of this account." Everything downstream hangs off Policy.

Act 6 — The loss (Domain 6: Claims)

September 10. Juan crashes tractor #7, injuring María. The Claim anchors everything. ClaimInvolvement records Juan as driver and María as third_party_injured — the flexible party-role model that replaced the old Driver entity. ClaimEvents form the timeline (FNOL → adjuster assigned → peritaje → reserve → settlement → payment → closed). ClaimCoverage ties the loss to the three triggered coverages (PD, TPL, PI). ClaimItem records the damage to tractor #7. Reserves are temporal — initial COP 75M, then revised to COP 100M after María's medical report, each a new record with its reason. Settlements (repair shop, María) and Payments (COP 42M, COP 50M) close the financial loop, with paid_to_party_id pointing back to Party.

Why this matters for the build: claims are the outcomes that validate or refute every UW and pricing decision. Temporal reserves let the actuary see development patterns ("initial reserves on collision claims run 20% light"). Per-coverage claim amounts let you compute "loss ratio on theft coverage across the whole book." And now the full combined-ratio chain is closed: Policy premium → Exposure earned → Claim incurred → Commission cost.

Act 7 — Turning documents into knowledge (Domain 7: Documents & Signals)

Every document in the story — submission, FNOL, peritaje, medical report, settlement — is an Artifact. The nightly pipeline runs four steps on each: DocumentClassification ("this is a peritaje", 96% confidence), Signal extraction (estimated_repair = COP 45M, damage_type = frontal_collision, each with a confidence, a concern_flag, and source_location so a human can verify), and DatasetResolution (this document belongs to ProductVersion 2024.1, RatingMatrix RM-2024.1).

The bitemporal trick lives here: each Signal carries valid_time (when the fact was true in the world) and ingested_time (when PLM learned it). That distinction is what makes Query 1.2 honest — "at bind, only signals with ingested_time <= bind_date were knowable."

Why this matters for the build: this is the extraction layer where 60–70% of the engineering risk lives. Signals are the atomic units the agents reason over; the controlled vocabulary and concern flags make them queryable; the provenance link makes them defensible.

Act 8 — The intelligence (Domain 8: Intelligence)

Monday morning, after the batch. The UW agent (an Actor with actor_kind = agent) produces an AgentOutput about the account, discovers a Pattern ("zone A + fleet >10 + age >7 → loss ratio 112%, lift 1.35×, n=48"), lists the 48 PatternMembers for drill-down, and issues a Recommendation ("request maintenance records before renewal" — with explicit reasoning and an explicit acknowledgment of what it can't see: the AON relationship, market pressure). That last structure is the Level-Two boundary, enforced in the schema, not just in the prompt.

Why this matters for the build: agent outputs are first-class nodes so the cross-domain agent can reason over them, and so disposition can record what the carrier did with each insight. "We flagged 15 patterns, they acted on 10, those segments improved 8 points" — that sentence is only provable because Pattern and Recommendation persist with disposition and outcome tracking. This is the evidence that PLM works.

Act 9 — Trust and the regulator (Domain 9: Provenance & Compliance)

Every machine-produced fact in the story has a ProvenanceNode (model, version, temperature, confidence, full prompt registered from day one). Every human action has an AuditEntry (Martín bound; Carlos set the reserve; the senior adjuster raised it). Low-confidence extractions become ReviewCases in the queue.

A year later the Superfinanciera sends an oficio about Juan's claim — a RegulatorRequest. The compliance officer assembles an EvidencePackage (Query 3.1 materialized): every artifact, signal, agent analysis, reserve with its evolution, and payment, each with its provenance, exported in one package. RetentionSchedule ensures nothing was deleted before Colombia's 10-year rule.

Note on the regulator: PLM does not connect to the regulator's systems. The oficio arrives by the carrier's normal channel; the compliance officer registers it and uploads it; PLM assembles the evidence; the human reviews and submits it. PLM's value is the assembly and reconstruction — days of work reduced to a click — not an integration.

A note on scope: multi-LOB vs multi-jurisdiction

The model is multi-LOB native: Product.line is a transversal dimension. Auto and SME coexist in one deployment, sharing Party/Account/Claim. Cross-LOB queries work.

The model is deliberately single-jurisdiction per deployment. A regional holding (AXA Colombia + AXA Brasil) gets two independent deployments — different regulator (SFC vs SUSEP), residency, retention. There is no shared substrate and no cross-country query, by permanent architectural decision (§ foundational doc). jurisdiction lives on Product, Container, and RetentionSchedule not to enable cross-border queries but so each deployment knows which regime it operates under. Cross-country consolidation, if the holding needs it, happens at corporate finance — outside PLM.

How the five core queries traverse this model

All five validate as concrete traversals (every hop is a real FK in this document):

  • 1.1 Account lifecycle — Account → Policies → (Items, Coverages, Transactions, Endorsements, Claims) → Artifacts → Signals, ordered in time.
  • 1.2 UW moment (bitemporal) — Policy → ProductVersion 2024.1 → (RatingMatrix, UWRuleset, Coverages) + Signals filtered to ingested_time <= bind_date.
  • 1.3 Relationship graph — Party → Account, Broker, PartyRelationships, ClaimInvolvements, Submission producers → the accumulation picture.
  • 2.2 Similar risks — new Submission's signals → similar historical Policies → Claims + Exposure → loss outcomes.
  • 3.1 Evidence package — Claim → Artifacts → Signals → ProvenanceNodes + Reserves + Settlements + Payments + AgentOutputs, bound to a RegulatorRequest.

Domain 1 — IDENTITY & PARTY

Purpose: Anchor identity for every person and organization that participates in the policy lifecycle. Separate identity (who they are) from role (what they do in a given context). Enable entity resolution, relationship traversal, and cross-domain queries.

Design principles: - Party is identity-only. Roles are contextual and live in the domains where they apply. - No LOB-specific subtypes (no Driver, no Vehicle Owner). The domain is product-agnostic. - Party-to-Party relationships are first-class typed edges, not subtypes (replaces Principal). - Aligned with ACORD Party/Role separation and 2023 virtual-party extension.

Entity: Party

What it is: The universal identity anchor. Every person or organization exists exactly once.

Why it exists: Entity resolution needs a single landing point. The relationship graph (Query 1.3) requires that the same entity connects across policies, claims, brokers, and accounts. Without Party as anchor, identity fragments across transactions.

In the example: AXA, AON, Transportes Andinos, Martín, Pedro, Juan, María — all are Parties.

Entity: Account

What it is: The commercial relationship between the carrier and a customer, through a distribution channel.

Why it exists: Query 1.1 is called "Account lifecycle reconstruction" — Account is the natural entry point for reconstructing everything that has happened with a customer. When Transportes Andinos has auto today and adds SME tomorrow, both policies live under one Account.

In the example: Transportes Andinos has an Account with AXA, placed through AON.

Domain 2 — PRODUCT & MASTER DATA

Purpose: Define the insurance products the carrier offers, their versioned configurations, available coverages, pricing structures, and underwriting rules. Enable bitemporal reconstruction (Query 1.2) by anchoring every policy, quote, and document to the specific product version in force at that moment.

Design principles: - Product is the stable concept; ProductVersion captures every change. - PLM does not replicate pricing engines or UW workbenches — it stores metadata about what was in force, not the execution logic. - The actual content (rating tables, appetite guidelines) lives as ReferenceDocuments in Containers. Entities here are structured metadata for resolution. - Coverage definitions are product-level (what's available and its parameters). Policy-level values live in PolicyCoverage (Domain: Policy Lifecycle). - Aligned with ACORD Product/Agreement model: Product defines what can be sold, Agreement (Policy) records what was sold.

In the example: AXA sells "Seguro de Automóviles" in Colombia. The current version defines 4 coverages (physical damage, third-party liability, theft, personal injury), a rating matrix with zone/age/use factors, and a UW ruleset with appetite guidelines. Transportes Andinos' policy was bound under a specific ProductVersion, rated by a specific RatingMatrix, and accepted under a specific UWRuleset.

Entity: Product

What it is: An insurance product the carrier offers. The stable identity of what is sold, independent of how it is configured over time.

Why it exists: Product is the transversal dimension described in the foundational document (§2.2). It tags every lifecycle artifact and organizes content within domain subjects. When the carrier expands from auto to SME, a new Product is created — the architecture doesn't change. Product enables queries like "show me all policies of this product" and "compare loss ratios across products."

In the example: "Seguro de Automóviles Colombia" is the Product. Its line is auto. If AXA later offers life insurance, that's a separate Product with line = life.

Domain 3 — CONTAINERS & REFERENCE

Purpose: Organize the carrier's institutional knowledge into containers that separate lifecycle evidence (what happened) from authored reference material (how to evaluate what happened). Provide the structure that the agents read from: lifecycle artifacts in the master container, reference material in domain subjects.

Design principles: - Two types of containers with fundamentally different governance: master container (continuous arrival, retention-governed) and domain subjects (deliberate authoring, version-governed). - ReferenceDocument is the authored knowledge — rating tables, appetite guides, reserve models. It bridges Domain 2 (structured metadata like RatingMatrix, UWRuleset) with the actual files. - Lifecycle artifacts (transactional documents: submissions, loss runs, FNOL reports) are Artifacts in Domain 7 (Documents & Signals), not ReferenceDocuments. The distinction is structural, not semantic. - Upload permissions follow the role-segmented model (§3.3) and are RBAC configuration, not data model entities.

In the example: AXA's PLM deployment has 4 containers: a Master Container (where Transportes Andinos' submission, policy document, and claim reports go), a Pricing Subject (where the rating matrix Excel lives), a UW Subject (where the appetite guidelines PDF lives), and a Claims Subject (where reserve models and adjuster playbooks live). The agents read from both sides to produce intelligence.

Domain 4 — QUOTE & APPLICATION

Purpose: Capture the pre-bind stage of the policy lifecycle: the submission of a risk for evaluation and the quote(s) produced in response. Preserve submissions that never become policies — they are essential for risk selection analysis and pattern detection.

Design principles: - Submission exists independently of Policy. Declined, withdrawn, and expired submissions are first-class citizens — they carry risk signals that inform underwriting patterns. - Multiple Quotes can exist per Submission (indicative, formal, counter-proposal). Each Quote records the terms offered at that point. - The Submission carries the risk profile as presented by the broker — this is the "input" that the UW agent reasons about. - Quote records which RatingMatrix produced the premium — critical for the bitemporal chain. - Separated from Policy Lifecycle because the pre-bind and post-bind stages have different governance and because not all submissions result in policies.

Why this domain exists separately from Policy Lifecycle: - Query 2.2 (similar risks) compares a NEW submission against BOTH bound policies AND historical submissions (including declined ones). Submissions must survive independently. - Query 2.1 (portfolio patterns) correlates quote-time signals with outcomes. The submission carries the quote-time signals. - A declined submission is evidence of appetite and risk selection discipline — "we said no to this profile, and here's why."

In the example: Pedro at AON submits a request to insure Transportes Andinos' 14 tractors. Martín evaluates and produces a quote at COP 85M using RatingMatrix 2024.1. Domain 5 — POLICY LIFECYCLE

Purpose: Represent the bound insurance contract and everything that happens to it: the items insured, the coverages applied, the earned exposure, and every transaction that modifies the contract over time. This is the core of the policy lifecycle that PLM memorializes.

Design principles: - Policy is the bound contract — it carries the terms agreed at bind, not the terms proposed (those live in Quote/Submission). - InsuredItem represents the physical or intangible objects being insured. Auto-specific fields are pragmatic for v1; LOB-agnostic fields ensure extensibility. - PolicyCoverage instantiates the product-level Coverage definition with policy-specific values (actual limits, deductibles, premium allocated). - Exposure enables loss ratio computation from primitives: earned_premium ÷ incurred_losses = loss ratio at any granularity. - PolicyTransaction + Endorsement provide the audit trail of every change. The Query 1.1 (account lifecycle reconstruction) walks this chain. - Renewal is modeled as a self-reference (prior_policy_id) — the renewal chain is a linked list of policies.

In the example: Transportes Andinos' policy is bound: 14 tractors, 4 coverages, COP 80M premium, AON as broker, Martín as binding underwriter. In June, 2 tractors are added via endorsement. Deliberately excluded from v1

The following concepts are relevant to the policy lifecycle but are excluded from PLM v1 by design. They may be addressed in v2+ if the pilot validates the need.

Reinsurance (reaseguro). Which portion of the risk was ceded to a reinsurer, under what treaty, at what ceded premium. Affects the carrier's net position but is an entire domain by itself (treaty, facultative, proportional, excess-of-loss). PLM v1 works with gross written premium and gross incurred losses. Net-of-reinsurance analytics are v2+. Rationale: the pilot carriers' CUO and actuary work with gross figures for the operational decisions PLM supports; net figures are a CFO/finance concern outside PLM's current scope.

Coinsurance (coaseguro). Whether the policy is shared with another carrier (leader/follower), at what participation percentage. Common in large commercial risks in LATAM but not typical in auto fleet. Out of scope for v1 auto.

Billing and collections (facturación y cobranza). Payment schedules, installments, premium collection status, overdue tracking, payment methods. This is operational data that lives in the carrier's core/PAS. PLM does not need to know if the client paid on time — that affects the carrier's cash flow, not the risk analysis PLM performs. If premium receivables become a signal for risk (clients who don't pay tend to have worse loss ratios), that would be a Signal extracted by an agent, not a Policy attribute.

Multiple named insureds per policy. A policy can have additional named insureds, certificate holders, loss payees. In auto fleet v1, the named insured is typically one organization (Transportes Andinos). For commercial property or liability, multiple named insureds are common. If needed in v2, this becomes a PolicyParty intersection entity: policy_id + party_id + party_role (named_insured, additional_insured, loss_payee, certificate_holder).

Per-item coverage granularity. Whether individual items can have different coverage configurations (tractor #7 has theft coverage but tractor #3 doesn't). In v1, PolicyCoverage is per-policy: all items share the same coverages. The workaround is InsuredItem.declared_value acting as a natural per-item limit for first-party coverages. If full per-item coverage is needed, a PolicyItemCoverage intersection entity (policy_id + insured_item_id + coverage_id + specific limits) would be added in v2.

Premium installment schedule. How the annual premium is split into payments (monthly, quarterly, annual). Related to billing, lives in the carrier's core. PLM tracks written_premium (annual total) and earned_premium (via Exposure, accrued over time), which is sufficient for loss ratio and combined ratio calculation.

Currency conversion. Multi-currency handling for carriers operating across borders. PLM v1 is single-country per deployment, so all amounts are in local currency. Multi-currency support (storing amounts in original currency + converted amount + exchange rate + conversion date) is v2+ if a carrier needs cross-border analytics.

Domain 6 — CLAIMS

Purpose: Capture everything that happens when a loss occurs: the claim itself, the parties involved, the items damaged, the coverages triggered, the reserves estimated, the settlements agreed, and the payments made. Claims are the outcomes that close the insurance cycle — the data that validates or refutes underwriting and pricing decisions.

Design principles: - Claim is the master record; all other entities hang off it. - ClaimInvolvement replaces the old Driver FK with a flexible party-role model — any party can be involved in any role. - Reserves are temporal: each adjustment is a new record, not an update. The reserve history tells the story of how understanding evolved. - The chain Claim → ClaimCoverage → PolicyCoverage → Coverage enables loss ratio by coverage type across the book. - The chain Claim → ClaimItem → InsuredItem enables loss analysis by item. - Settlement and Payment are separate: settlement is the agreement, payment is the execution. A settlement can produce multiple payments to different parties.

In the example: Juan crashes tractor #7, injuring María. The claim triggers 3 coverages (PD, TPL, PI), involves 2 parties (Juan as driver, María as injured third party), damages 1 item (tractor #7), evolves through 2 reserve adjustments, produces 2 settlements, and results in payments to the repair shop and to María.

Domain 7 — DOCUMENTS & SIGNALS

Purpose: Represent the lifecycle artifacts (documents) that arrive through the ingestion pipeline, the structured knowledge extracted from them (signals), the classifications assigned, and the resolution that connects each artifact to its correct product/version context. This is where PLM converts unstructured data into queryable knowledge.

Design principles: - Artifact is a lifecycle document (transactional) — distinct from ReferenceDocument (Domain 3, authored reference material). Both live in Containers but have different governance. - Classification, Signal extraction, and Resolution are outputs of the ingestion pipeline (Layer 1, §3.3). Each carries confidence and provenance. - Signals are the atomic units of structured knowledge that agents reason about. They are PLM's primary value extraction. - Document state (in_process → pending_review → graph_ready) tracks each artifact through the pipeline. - Signals are bitemporal: valid_time (when the fact was true in the real world) and ingested_time (when PLM learned it).

In the example: Over the lifecycle of Transportes Andinos' policy and Juan's claim, dozens of documents arrive: the submission package, loss runs, the policy document, the endorsement for 2 new tractors, the FNOL report, the peritaje report, María's medical report, the settlement agreement. Each becomes an Artifact. From each, the pipeline extracts Signals that the agents use to reason, detect patterns, and produce insights.

Domain 8 — INTELLIGENCE

Purpose: Capture the outputs of PLM's AI agents as first-class entities in the graph. Agent analyses, discovered patterns, pattern members, and structured recommendations persist as queryable knowledge — not ephemeral text. This enables agents to reason about other agents' outputs, carriers to track which insights they acted on, and the cross-domain loop to close.

Design principles: - Agent outputs are first-class graph nodes, not transient messages. They persist, they're queryable, they carry provenance. - Patterns are statistical discoveries with measurable lift. They're not opinions — they have sample sizes and can be validated against outcomes. - Recommendations follow Level Two discipline: concrete suggestion + explicit reasoning + acknowledgment of factors the system does not see. Entity: Pattern

What it is: A statistical pattern discovered by an agent — a combination of conditions that correlates with a measurable outcome. Not an opinion; a data-backed finding with lift, sample size, and confidence.

Why it exists: Patterns are PLM's highest-value output. "Zone A + fleet >10 + vehicle age >7 → LR 112% (1.35x lift, n=48)" is actionable intelligence the actuary can use to adjust pricing. Domain 9 — PROVENANCE & COMPLIANCE

Purpose: Ensure every piece of knowledge PLM produces can answer "how was this made?" (provenance) and every regulatory question can answer "why did we do this?" (compliance). This is the trust and accountability layer that makes PLM auditable, reproducible, and regulatorily defensible.

Design principles: - Provenance is first-class graph nodes, not embedded metadata (per §3.2). Every knowledge-producing operation creates a ProvenanceNode. - Medium provenance exposed in UI in v1 (source, model, version, confidence, timestamp). Complete provenance (full prompt, retrieval context, reasoning trace) registered in graph from day one, exposed in UI in v2. - Compliance entities support the third dimension of PLM's value: regulatory compliance and audit reconstruction. - AuditEntry captures human actions. ProvenanceNode captures system/AI actions. Together they provide complete accountability. - EvidencePackage is the materialization of Query 3.1 — the structured evidence for a decision or regulatory response.

In the example: Every signal extracted from Juan's claim documents has a ProvenanceNode. When the Superfinanciera asks about the claim, the compliance officer assembles an EvidencePackage with complete traceability. The RetentionSchedule ensures nothing is deleted before the 10-year Colombian requirement.

Entity: ProvenanceNode

What it is: A first-class record of how a piece of knowledge was produced. Created every time the system classifies a document, extracts a signal, resolves a version, or produces an agent output.

When the compliance officer clicks on a signal and asks "where did this come from?", the ProvenanceNode answers completely: what model, what version, what temperature, what confidence, what inputs, when. Appendix — The five core queries, validated against the model

Each query is a concrete graph traversal. Every hop below corresponds to a real foreign key in this document (validated programmatically). Below each, what it returns for Transportes Andinos.

Query 1.1 — Account lifecycle reconstruction

Question: "Show me everything that happened with Transportes Andinos, in time order."

Traversal: Account → Policy (account_id) → InsuredItem, PolicyCoverage, PolicyTransaction, Endorsement, Claim (all policy_id) → Artifact (policy_id, claim_id) → Signal (artifact_id), ordered by date.

Returns for the example: - Feb 2024 — Submission (Pedro via AON) → Quote COP 80M → Bind (Martín) - Mar 2024 — Policy active, 14 tractors, 4 coverages - Jun 2024 — Endorsement: +2 tractors, premium → COP 91.4M - Sep 2024 — Claim CLM-2024-00089 (tractor #7) - Sep–Nov 2024 — FNOL → peritaje → reserves → settlements → payments

Each event carries its Artifacts, Signals, and the Actor who executed it. 12 hops, all valid.

Query 1.2 — Underwriting moment reconstruction (bitemporal)

Question: "At bind (15 Feb 2024), what was knowable?"

Traversal: Policy → ProductVersion 2024.1 → RatingMatrix RM-2024.1, UWRuleset UW-2024.1, Coverages; Signals filtered to ingested_time <= bind_date.

Returns for the example: "At bind, Martín had: 14 tractors, average age 8, zone A, 2 prior claims, under UW-2024.1 ('fleets >10 in zone A require senior review'). Premium computed via RM-2024.1." The uw_rationale field captures his reasoning. Signals from September's accident are excluded because their ingested_time is after the bind. 11 hops, all valid. This bitemporal filter is what makes the reconstruction defensible to a regulator.

Query 1.3 — Relationship graph

Question: "How does Transportes Andinos connect to the rest of the book?"

Traversal: Party → Account, Broker, PartyRelationship (from/to), ClaimInvolvement, Submission.producer_party_id.

Returns for the example: Account → other policies of the same insured (auto + SME if present); Broker AON → other accounts AON placed; PartyRelationship → group companies if Transportes Andinos is a subsidiary; ClaimInvolvement → Juan as driver across claims; Pedro as producer across submissions. Reveals accumulation: "AON has 18 fleet accounts, 5 in zone A; Pedro originated 12." 9 hops, all valid.

Query 2.2 — Similar risks

Question: "A new fleet submission in zone A arrives. Which historical policies resemble it, and what happened?"

Traversal: new Submission's Signals → similar historical Policies (graph + vector search) → Claim, Reserve (incurred), Exposure (earned) → loss ratio per match.

Returns for the example: "Of 48 policies similar to this submission, average LR was 112%. Transportes Andinos, the closest match, is at 190% LR in year one." This connects a specific decision to historical outcomes — the highest immediate-value query. 7 hops, all valid.

Query 3.1 — Evidence package

Question: "The Superfinanciera asks about Juan's claim. Assemble the evidence."

Traversal: EvidencePackage → Claim → Artifacts → Signals → ProvenanceNodes; Claim → Reserves (75M → 100M with reasons) → Settlements → Payments (42M shop, 50M María); AgentOutput → ProvenanceNode; all bound to RegulatorRequest (oficio SFC 2025-0342).

Returns for the example: a structured, exportable package where every piece shows how it was produced. What takes the compliance officer days of cross-system searching, PLM assembles in one click. 12 hops, all valid.

The three optional queries (2.1 portfolio patterns, 3.2 pricing signals, cross-domain) are supported by Pattern, PatternMember, and AgentOutput (Domain 8), all validated as connected.
